## Overview

This ongoing project investigates how high-density surface electrode arrays can be computationally optimized for selective stimulation of targeted muscle regions.

The framework combines finite-element modeling, numerical optimization, and machine learning to determine how current should be distributed across independently controlled electrodes to concentrate stimulation within a desired muscle target while reducing off-target activation.

The current phase focuses on developing and validating a standardized computational framework. Long-term work will progressively incorporate more realistic tissue anatomy and adapt the optimization architecture to specific body regions and stimulation scenarios.

---

## High-Density Stimulation Framework

The initial model uses an 8×8 surface electrode array with 64 independently controlled electrodes positioned above a multilayer tissue volume.

Muscle tissue is represented using anisotropic conductivity, allowing the model to account for the dependence of electrical conduction on muscle-fiber orientation.

Rather than evaluating every possible multi-electrode stimulation pattern directly in COMSOL, the framework precomputes the electric field generated by each individual electrode under a unit-current condition.

These basis fields can then be combined through linear superposition to efficiently reconstruct the field generated by arbitrary 64-channel current distributions.

---

## Basis-Field Simulation

For each muscle-fiber orientation, a set of electrode-specific basis fields is generated:

- 64 independently activated electrodes;
- multiple muscle-fiber orientations;
- anisotropic muscle conductivity;
- standardized multilayer tissue geometry.

The current Phase 1 design evaluates 18 fiber orientations, resulting in 1,152 finite-element basis simulations.

Once these basis fields are generated, new stimulation configurations can be evaluated computationally without rerunning a complete finite-element simulation for every candidate current distribution.

---

## Current-Steering Optimization

The optimization stage searches for a 64-dimensional electrode-current vector that selectively concentrates the electric field within a target muscle region.

Target regions are varied across spatial location and depth, while muscle-fiber orientation is also varied to capture the directional electrical properties of muscle tissue.

The optimization objective balances three competing goals:

1. maximizing stimulation within the target region;
2. minimizing off-target electric-field exposure;
3. maintaining feasible and regularized electrode-current distributions.

This creates a systematic mapping between target geometry, muscle orientation, and optimized high-density stimulation patterns.

---

## Machine-Learning Surrogate Model

Numerical optimization can generate high-quality stimulation configurations, but solving a new optimization problem for every target condition may be computationally expensive.

To enable rapid prediction, the optimized solutions are used to train an inverse machine-learning model.

The initial formulation maps a compact description of the stimulation target and muscle orientation

**(target position, target depth, fiber orientation)**

to a predicted

**64-channel electrode-current distribution.**

This creates a surrogate model capable of approximating optimization-derived stimulation strategies without repeatedly solving the full numerical optimization problem.

---

## Phase 1 Design

The standardized Phase 1 framework includes:

- 8×8 independently controlled surface electrodes;
- 18 muscle-fiber orientations;
- 800 target regions per orientation;
- 1,152 finite-element basis simulations;
- 14,400 optimization problems;
- approximately 15,000 optimized stimulation samples for machine-learning development.

This first stage is intended to establish the feasibility, numerical behavior, and predictive performance of the overall FEM–optimization–ML architecture before introducing additional anatomical complexity.

---

## Toward Anatomically Informed Stimulation

The standardized model is designed as a starting point rather than a complete representation of clinical anatomy.

Future stages will progressively incorporate:

- heterogeneous tissue conductivity;
- spatially varying muscle-fiber architecture;
- realistic muscle and limb geometry;
- additional anatomical structures such as nerves, fascia, and vascular tissue;
- variable target shapes and sizes;
- body-region-specific tissue and electrode configurations.

The long-term goal is to retain a general computational optimization architecture while adapting its physical parameters and anatomical representation to specific stimulation sites.

---

## Current Research Questions

- How effectively can independently controlled high-density electrodes concentrate stimulation within a desired muscle region?
- How do muscle-fiber orientation and target depth affect optimal current-steering patterns?
- Can precomputed basis fields and linear superposition provide an efficient alternative to repeated full FEM simulations?
- Can machine-learning surrogate models reproduce optimization-derived electrode-current distributions with sufficient accuracy?
- How should a standardized optimization framework be adapted as increasingly realistic anatomy is introduced?

---

## Research Direction

The broader goal of this project is to move from physics-based simulation toward computationally efficient and anatomically informed stimulation planning.

By integrating finite-element modeling, numerical optimization, and machine learning, the project aims to develop a scalable framework for translating desired stimulation targets into optimized high-density electrode configurations.
