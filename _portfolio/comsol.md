## Overview

This project investigates the safety and effectiveness of neurostimulation systems using finite-element modeling (COMSOL). The goal is to understand how stimulation parameters and electrode configurations influence electric field distribution and tissue heating, and to enable safe and targeted stimulation in practical neuroengineering applications.

The work combines physics-based simulation with data-driven analysis to bridge modeling and system-level optimization.

---

## My Contributions

- Developed multi-layer finite-element models of biological tissue to simulate electric field distribution and thermal effects during electrical stimulation.
- Analyzed the impact of stimulation parameters (current amplitude, pulse width, frequency, duration) on temperature rise and safety constraints.
- Evaluated high-density grid electrode designs and compared them with conventional single-electrode configurations under equivalent stimulation conditions.
- Studied spatial current distribution across electrode arrays to understand selectivity and coverage in muscle stimulation scenarios.
- Generated large-scale simulation datasets across parameter sweeps and electrode configurations.
- Applied machine learning models (e.g., random forest, neural networks) to identify optimal electrode subsets for targeted stimulation.

---

## Key Findings

- Grid electrode arrays can achieve thermal safety comparable to single-electrode designs under appropriate parameter settings.
- Electrode geometry and stimulation parameters significantly affect both spatial selectivity and thermal behavior.
- Data-driven methods can efficiently explore large configuration spaces and identify effective stimulation strategies.

---

## Takeaway

This project demonstrates how finite-element modeling and data-driven approaches can be integrated to design safe, effective, and scalable neurostimulation systems.
